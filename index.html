    Scan testing & FAQ

    QR Studio never claims a code was physically scanned for you. Always test before publishing — here is how.

    4

    How to test your QR code

    Sixty seconds, one phone.

    1. Download it. Use the Download panel and grab a PNG (or SVG) at the size you plan to publish.
    2. Open your phone camera. On iOS use the Camera app, on Android use the Camera or Google Lens.
    3. Scan the exported file. Show the file on another screen and scan it, or print it and scan the print.
    4. Verify the decoded content. Check the link, phone number, Wi-Fi details or event data is exactly right.

    Test at the final physical size. A QR code that works on a 512px screen may fail when printed small. Aim for at least 2 cm × 2 cm for short links.

    5

    Scanning best practices

    Small choices, big difference.

    • Keep the foreground much darker than the background — high contrast scans best.
    • Never invert a QR code (light modules on dark) unless you have tested it on several phones.
    • Leave a quiet zone of at least 2 modules (4 is the standard) around the code.
    • Use error correction Q or H when you add a logo or expect print wear.
    • Keep logo coverage at or below 25% of the QR area and never cover the three corner eyes.
    • Export at 1024px or larger for print; 2048px for posters and banners.

    QR Studio checks contrast, quiet zone and logo coverage for you, but those are estimates. A final physical scan is still the only real test.

    PNG is a raster image — perfect for social media, apps and quick sharing. SVG is vector markup built from paths, so it stays razor sharp at any size and is the best choice for print. Both include your colors, shapes and logo.

    A logo covers real modules. Error correction lets scanners rebuild a portion of the code: level M recovers about 15%, Q about 25% and H about 30%. That is why QR Studio recommends Q or H when a logo is enabled, warns above 20% coverage, and clears the covered modules to the background color for a clean look.

    Scanners need contrast between dark modules and light background. QR Studio computes the WCAG contrast ratio and warns below 4.5:1, with a much louder warning below 2:1 — you can still export, because you decide what your scanner can read. Inverted codes (white modules on a dark background) are flagged too because some older scanners refuse to read them.

    Saved QR codes and recent activity are stored in LocalStorage, so clearing site data or using private browsing wipes them. Download anything important. If LocalStorage is blocked, the studio still works and tells you that saving is unavailable.

    Wi-Fi codes use the standard WIFI:T:…;S:…;P:…;H:…;; string, contact cards use vCard 3.0, events use an iCalendar VEVENT, SMS uses SMSTO:, phone uses tel:, email uses mailto:, WhatsApp uses the official wa.me link format and locations use geo: coordinates or a Google Maps search URL.
